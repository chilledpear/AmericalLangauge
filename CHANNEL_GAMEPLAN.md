# Channel Gameplan

Faceless, AI-voiced YouTube channel in the "IT girl" space. Goal: YouTube ad revenue first, digital product later. No face, no voice.

---

## 1. The channel in one line

**Faceless, AI-voiced self-development essays for ambitious women, covering power, money, status and dating.**

"IT girl" is the visual brand. The topics underneath it (dating, money, status, power) are what viewers actually search for.

---

## 2. Where the channel stands now

- 4 videos in the first 2 days, 2-10 views each. That is normal for a new channel with no audience history. It does not mean the topics failed.
- The four videos cover four different audiences (dating a rich man, fat loss, feminine energy, marrying up). YouTube can't tell who to show them to.
- Drop the fat-loss direction. Different audience, plus health-claim risk.
- Judge the channel after **4-6 weeks and 15-20 videos** in a tighter lane, not before.

---

## 3. Niche decision

**Lead lane: dating and marrying up, plus money and status psychology for women.**

Why this lane:
- Higher ad value than pure beauty/aesthetic content (relative claim; confirm with your own Studio RPM data).
- Plenty of source material for AI scripting (books, studies, biographies, Reddit threads).
- Works as illustrated/voiceover essays, so it fits a faceless channel.
- Natural digital products later: money-habits workbook, dating standards guide, etiquette/"old money" playbook, planners.

Old money is a steady, mature aesthetic, not a breakout trend. Use it as one angle (for example the backlash angle), not the headline.

---

## 4. Two series (the format)

Based on outlier videos reviewed (the 48 Laws for Women video had ~418K views).

### Series A (backbone): "[Famous book or framework] explained for women"
- No footage needed, so it fits a faceless channel.
- Structure: counterintuitive hook, promise to cover everything, split into numbered acts, each law/lesson gets a recognizable question, a famous-person example, one named psychology concept, a "this isn't manipulation" reframe, and a takeaway.
- Candidates: *The Art of Seduction*, *48 Laws of Power*, *Psychology of Money*, *Atomic Habits*, *Mastery*, each "for women".

### Series B: a famous woman's story proves a counterintuitive thesis
- Structure: hook, origin story, struggle, a real quote, the lesson, three takeaways, closing line.
- Use illustrations, not other people's footage or photos.
- Candidates: Jackie Kennedy, Coco Chanel, Grace Kelly (fact-check biographical details).

Avoid news-led, event-dependent videos as a core format. They spike and fade.

---

## 5. Trend layer (use, don't depend on)

Phrases seen in dating and finance content on TikTok in 2026. Sources are mostly blogs and glossaries, so verify before building on them.

- **Money:** girl math, moneymaxxing, loud budgeting, doom spending, underconsumption core, money manifestation.
- **Dating:** clear-coding, hardballing, beige flags, micro-dating, choremance, chatfishing, "dating recession".

Rules:
- A phrase is not a topic. Put the phrase in the title, but build the video on a bigger question people actually search.
- Girl math has the most proof (years of data, ongoing discussion, built-in critique angle).

---

## 6. Weekly workflow

1. **Outlier research (30 min):** find 15-20 channels in the niche, especially small ones. Sort by Popular. Note videos far above that channel's usual views: topic, title formula, thumbnail style.
2. **Validate demand:** YouTube Studio -> Analytics -> Research tab, YouTube search autocomplete (incognito), Google Trends, TikTok Creative Center, Pinterest Trends, Reddit.
3. **Gather 3-5 real sources** per video (a book summary, a study, a biography, discussion threads).
4. **Draft with AI:** outline with hook, 4-6 beats, closing takeaway.
5. **Rewrite the hook and ending by hand.** That is where retention is won.
6. **Fact-check every claim.** Name the real study or cut the claim.
7. **Build the thumbnail:** 3-5 words max, one focal point, consistent template.

---

## 7. Cadence and testing

- Post **1-2 videos per week**, not 4 in a day.
- Make about 5 videos per series, then compare **impressions click-through rate** and **average view duration** in Studio (not just views).
- Whichever series has the best retention and click-through gets the next batch of 10.
- Before copying any outlier format, open the channel and check its last 20 videos. If only one hit in 20, it was a lucky topic, not a reliable format.

## 9. Guardrails

- **YouTube inauthentic-content rules:** every video needs a real point of view and a different structure from the last. Don't mass-produce templated videos.
- **No invented personal stories** for the AI narrator ("my grandfather built a business..."). It erodes trust and risks policy problems.
- **Fact-check psychology and money claims.** Scripts like the ones reviewed contained shaky claims ("fear and excitement are neurologically identical", "the most attractive women get approached least"). Name the study or drop it.
- **Don't use other people's photos or footage** in videos or thumbnails. Stylized illustrations keep you consistent with your look and lower copyright risk.
- **Don't copy other creators' scripts.** Synthesize multiple sources and write your own framing.
